# Sources

Every dataset, tool and asset used in "All Gear, No Go". The same sources are listed in the Sources section at the bottom of the live page, and the exact caption credit for each chart is in `CAPTION-CREDITS.md`.

## 1. Data sources

### Caravan Industry Association of Australia — State of the Industry 2025
- Link: https://www.caravanindustry.com.au
- Used for: caravan and camping trips, nights, spending, economic impact, accommodation types, occupancy and revenue by site type, age groups, and the top tourism regions (Table 2).
- Files: `data/national_decade.csv`, `data/spend_series.csv`, `data/occupancy.csv`, `data/accommodation.csv`, `data/accommodation_waffle.csv`, `data/age_groups.csv`, `data/regions.csv`, `data/national_2025.csv`, `data/campervans_national.csv`
- Charts: 01, 02, 03, 04, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16

### BITRE — Road Vehicles Australia (via data.gov.au)
- Links: https://www.bitre.gov.au and https://data.gov.au
- Used for: registered campervans and all registered vehicles by state, postcode and ABS remoteness class, motor vehicle census as at January 2021–2025 (103,481 campervans across 2,414 postcodes in 2025).
- Files: `data/rva-2025-mvs-vehtype-streg-poareg-rpc.csv` (raw download), `data/campervans_state.csv`, `data/campervans_postcode.csv`, `data/campervans_cells.csv` (postcodes pooled into 1-degree squares), `data/postcode_centroids.csv`
- Charts: 05, 06, 07, 11, 15, 17
- Note: BITRE records where a vehicle is registered, not where it is used or who owns it, so rental depots inflate some places (for example Tasmania).

### OpenStreetMap contributors
- Link: https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright (data licence: Open Database Licence, ODbL)
- Extracted on 7 October 2026 through the Overpass API (https://overpass-api.de): 9,945 camp and caravan sites with coordinates (7,920 camp sites and 2,025 caravan sites).
- Files: `data/export.geojson` (raw extract), `data/campgrounds.csv`, `data/campgrounds_cells.csv` (0.9-degree cells), `data/campgrounds_hex.geojson` (equal-area hexagons)
- Charts: 08, 09, 10
- Note: OpenStreetMap is mapped by volunteers, so counts reflect mapping effort as well as real supply. The map credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors" appears in each caption.

### Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS)
- Link: https://www.abs.gov.au
- Used for: population by state and territory (March 2026), used for per-head rates, and the remoteness structure (ASGS remoteness classes) used to group postcodes.
- Files: population figures are in `data/states.csv`; remoteness classes are in `data/campervans_postcode.csv`
- Charts: 06, 10, 11, 12, 17

### State and territory boundaries — rowanhogan/australian-states
- Link: https://github.com/rowanhogan/australian-states
- Used for: state outlines on every map, simplified and converted to GeoJSON and TopoJSON for this project.
- Files: `data/au_states.geojson`, `data/au_states.topojson`
- Charts: 05, 08, 09, 10, 14

## 2. Derived data

Calculated from the sources above by script before the charts load:

- Index with 2015 = 100 (chart 01) and year-on-year change in spending (chart 02)
- Spend per trip and average nights per trip (chart 03)
- Growth in registered campervans since 2021 (chart 15)
- Campervans per 10,000 registered vehicles, by state, remoteness class and 1-degree square (charts 05, 06, 07, 17)
- Campgrounds per 100,000 residents (chart 10) and campground counts per hexagon or cell (charts 08, 09)
- Trip shares rounded to 100 squares for the waffle chart (chart 13)

## Note on data paths in the chart specifications

Every `.vg.json` in `/specs` loads its data from an absolute URL:

    https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Nisarg-bit/fit3179-dv2/main/data/<file>

raw.githubusercontent.com serves these with `access-control-allow-origin: *`, so a
spec can be pasted straight into the Vega Editor (vega.github.io/editor) and will
render — relative paths such as `data/states.csv` would not resolve there.

Consequence to be aware of: these URLs hard-code the repository name and the `main`
branch. Renaming the repo or the default branch breaks every chart.
