01

The boom that was

Australians took 15.2 million caravan and camping trips in 2024, up from 11.0 million a decade earlier — a rise of 38%. The money grew faster still: the sector’s economic impact (a broader measure than the $10.6 billion visitors spent directly) reached $27.1 billion, against $19.0 billion in 2015. Campervan registrations climbed in every state and territory, from 83,944 in 2021 to 103,481 by January 2025. On almost every headline measure, the past ten years look like an unbroken run of growth.

02

The turn

The nights tell a different story. Trips grew 38% over the decade but nights only 15%, and the average stay shrank from 4.1 nights in 2020 to 3.78 in 2024. Spending per trip rose from $546 to $712 — and then stopped. Total expenditure peaked at $10.7 billion in 2023 and slipped to $10.6 billion in 2024, the first fall in the series. Occupancy fell across every site type that year. People are still going; they are paying more and staying less.

03

Where the vans are

The 103,481 campervans on the register are spread across 2,414 postcodes, and they are not a city habit. Ownership runs at 38 per 10,000 vehicles in the major cities and rises to 62.5 in the outer regions — then collapses to 29.8 in Very Remote Australia, the lowest of the five classes. Vans cluster where there is road access to country, not in the deep interior. One caveat matters: registration records where a vehicle is registered, not where its owner lives. Two depot postcodes sit at forty and thirty times the median rate, and ten of the eighteen highest are Tasmanian — hire fleets, not households.

04

Where the campgrounds are

OpenStreetMap lists 9,945 places to camp in Australia — 7,920 camp sites and 2,025 caravan sites. They crowd the east coast, the south-west and Tasmania, and inland they thin into strings that follow the highways. Counted per 100,000 residents the picture inverts: the Northern Territory reaches 150 against New South Wales’ 27, because the divisor is people and there are few. Read this as where campgrounds are mapped rather than where they are — OpenStreetMap is contributor-built, and remote areas have fewer contributors.

05

A territory of destinations

Across the six states, owning a van and using one go together: more RVs per head means more camping nights per head, with a correlation of +0.76. Tasmania leads on both. The Northern Territory breaks the pattern, and breaks it hard — 3.0 RVs per 1,000 residents, the fewest in the country, but 3,689 camping nights per 1,000, by far the most. Territorians are not the ones camping. The Northern Territory is a destination rather than an ownership market, and it is the only place where the two come apart.

06

What they actually book

Most caravan and camping trips involve neither a caravan nor camping. Cabins take 39% of trips; only 23% are actual camping, and just 6% use a motorhome or campervan. Older travellers stay longest — the over-55s take 31% of trips but 42% of nights. Where people go is shifting too: Queensland Country drew 34% more trips and 52% more nights in 2024, while the Hunter took 17% more trips but 33% fewer nights.
